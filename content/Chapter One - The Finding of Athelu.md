#### Chapter One: The Finding of Athelu

In days long past an event simply called "The Rain" caused every living being to shelter itself inside caves, ruins, or anything that would keep them dry. For if they didn't, an ocean's worth of water pelting from the sky would wash them away to their dooms. The Rain lasted years upon years, and ravaged the world beneath it. Anything from dirt to stone was carved and wrought, and when it stopped, and the people of Odacia looked and saw, before them lied an unrecognizable mess. For the Agaricans, that being the Red Capped Mushroom Men who emerged from an ancient astrologer's ruins, they saw much of the same. Immediately however, their king by blood Calder D. Lucidum who waited long for the day to escape those ruins had hope and a plan. He and his people would go to The Greatwood Gadfyrth, which was the home of the Agaricans before The Rain ever began in order to collect materials or even rebuild their home if it wasn't too ravaged. Those woods lied just West of the ruins in which they dwelt, so Calder had his men march as soon as possible. But first he had a special order for his elderly scribe and close friend named Dallin J. Clathrus, so he called Dallin into his quarters and spoke to him saying,

"Dallin my friend, I have seen the sun and the clouds, it is time for our people to be free of the shackles of these ruins. But for you I have a special quest, since it is that you are a scribe, I request you would go ahead of us and warn us if anything is wrong in the Greatwood." Dallin would reply saying, "Of course Calder! It is a blessing upon me that you would request such a thing." "Excellent my friend, I will supply you with a week's worth of food, and a weapon if you'd need it."

And so Dallin was given these things including a dagger made from some sort of black metal designed by an astrologer, then he grabbed his staff, and went to walk seeing the damages that had been done to the world. Now, he only knew of the beauty of the past world from stories and writings, for The Rain began many years before him, or his father, or his father's father were born, but still the sight saddened him. Where once grassy rolling hills lied, now dwell mudslides that lead into unescapable bogs, and the grass he saw was more often then not drowned or ripped from the ground. Carefully he went on his way through the muddy hills Westward towards his mission, and found it unpleasant and difficult to pass those hills without slipping into those muddy bogs. Even so, he made his way and found himself in a flatter plain, he no longer feared being encased in an earthy grave, but was still assaulted by the sight of dead land and mud everywhere. Still he had no choice but to move on and on for a few days, until he passed a hill and saw something that he at first thought was an illusion or dream, but as he stared it didn't go away and he didn't wake up. In front of him was the edge of the Greatwood, its trees were still there and they looked untouched, and so did the ground beneath them. He fell silent for a while, and thought it must've been divine providence that it would be him standing there to see this first, and he rejoiced throwing his hands in their air and yelling praises in Shroomen's tongue. His methodic walk he had done for most his journey had turned into a full sprint as he rushed and jumped into those woods, around him he spotted not just plants but also animals who were just as spooked by him as he was excited to see them. After frolicking more then anyone of his people had in generations, he began to calm himself and sat in a little grove taking in the view with great contentedness. Though as he examined his surroundings he saw a strange trail in the distance, so he picked himself up and walked to see what it was. As he got close he saw a trail of destruction like something large rampaged through the forest breaking as many trees as possible within its path, and in the middle of the trail before him he saw a large blue scale about the size of his hand on the ground. He was vexed and frightened by what he saw, no manuscript or tale he had ever heard referred to some large blue-scaled creature, to him this was something new and quite unwelcome. He swiftly pocketed the scale and found that the day was beginning to end, so he searched for a spot far away from that trail of destruction of which he deemed safe in order to sleep the night. While he slept he connected with the ground, and in the ground was mycelium, the very same mycelium that his ancestors used before The Rain, and there it stayed alive after all this time. In Dallin's sleep the mycelium spoke to him first in a way of imagery and feelings, safety, life giving, protection. His mind filled with pictures of the woods like instructions to a specific spot, and before he could see where it leads it ended before a hill and a word was spoken in many voices, and it said, "Athelu." His dreams went silent the rest of that night, but when he woke in the morning he had realized he was the first of the Agaricans or possibly even all other races of the Shroomen to connect with their mycelium in an age, and of all the things he had heard from the mycelium, he knew to obey the vision and walked in a path following the imagery he saw in his head. Passing tree, rock, and ruin he found the very same hill that blocked his view in his dream, so he passed it and beheld in front of him a large red tree with smooth bark and hanging green leaves, from its roots flowed a yellow sap with a sweet aroma, and he knew this was The Athelu that the mycelium meant to show him, and it was magnificent. He thought it a thing of beauty, and had never felt so blessed in all his years in those ruins. Knowing now what the Agaricans must focus on, he began to trek back Eastward to those rolling mudslides to inform Calder what he had found. Meanwhile during Dallin's journey, Calder already had he and his people, of which there was about two-thousand, begin to march through the mud-soaked hills. They made it about halfway through the hills before Calder noticed morale was low, the uncounted years in those ruins had done a number on his people down to their core. That combined with their numbers ensured they traveled slowly across the hills. As they continued onward however, Calder spotted Dallin swiftly rushing over to his people, and his heart dropped awaiting what Dallin had to say. When it was that Dallin got close he pulled Calder to the side and spoke to him,

"Calder! Calder it's better then I have could've imagined!" Calder's worry changed to excitement and he said, "How? Is the forest still there?" "Yes! Even more so, it's untouched, and our mycelium net still lives after all this time! There's life there just like in the writings and stories, it must be a blessing upon us, I'm sure of it!" Calder slapped Dallin on the back and said, "Hah! That alone is more then I could have ever hoped for, I will inform our people, then we will march! "Hold on though, there's more." "Yes?" "In the heart of the forest there's a tree named Athelu, according to our mycelium net, which spoke to me in my sleep. I think that it has something to do with how the forest is being kept alive, we should head there to set up our home I'd reckon." "It sounds like we should Dallin, and it'll be you that leads us there. Now prepare to move!"

Dallin nodded his head, and Calder climbed up to the tallest hill he could find to speak, and he said,

"Hark! My people, I know the mud here looks dreary but do not despair! We are free from our bonds and we now cannot falter! I know this because Dallin, my scribe, has informed me that the Greatwood Gadfyrth still lives! We will go there immediately, we will take back our home, and the Agaricans will become a mighty people again! Come now, let us march!"

The spirits of the Agaricans were then lifted, and they all moved with a swift pace Westward. It did not take them long before they reached the edge of the forest and once they were there Calder looked to his sons Orman the youngest, Harman, and Rodman the oldest, who were all fully grown men, and he spoke to them saying,

"Listen now my sons, it is time for you to prove yourselves, because now that the world is open to our people again you have duties to attend to just as I do. You are the princes of the Agaricans, and I would ask that you would take five-hundred men and establish a perimeter around the forest. Would you be ready for that?" First Rodman spoke eagerly, "Of course father!" Then spoke Orman, "I would have it no other way." Then Harman, who took a moment to speak, "Perhaps, but what would we do if trouble comes our way? We've only got simple supplies and barely any tools to defend ourselves." Calder said to him, "Firstly, if any true danger arrives I would have you and all the men come to us in the center of the forest immediately. But I also expect you all to be able to hold yourselves if it came to that. And if you're worried that you'll not know what to do, I've been told by Dallin that our mycelium net still lives. And from what I've been told that means the knowledge of our ancestors is available to all of us as long as we connect with it. And even if it doesn't, I'll hand you and your brothers a few manuscripts that were written for this exact situation. I know you'll be able to manage on your own Harman, so if you and your brothers work together you will be unstoppable." Harman's eyes lit up at the idea of learning from the mycelium net, and gave his response, "Yes then father. I'll go with my brothers." Calder nodded and said, "Then it will be done."

So it was arranged that five-hundred men, a few manuscripts, and a fitting proportion of supplies were to be handed to his sons. After that was done Calder and his men delved deeper into the forest, while the princes established their home. They marched and marched through Gadfyrth until they too found Athelu, and all who saw it was awed at the sight just as Dallin had been, and it was made that the king and other noble peoples were to be placed at the foot of the tree, and others would radiate away from them in a circle from there. They had no tents, and night fell before they could scrap together any shelter. So all the Agaricans slept with no roof, but it did not fully matter, for all the Agaricans from Calder and his men to the princes and their men slept standing, and from their feet they finally reconnected with their mycelium, and it was like a great reawakening. In their heads they were taught how to do things like farm, hunt, mine, and smith. Anything they'd need to repair their kingdom. Then their bodies were rejuvenated, and healed from the weakness that possessed them over the years spent sealed away from the world. While everyone else had that experience however, it was different for Calder. The mycelium talked to him separately, and he dreamt of rain, and the Greatwood being damaged by it, yet somehow it was healed so that it would not die. Then he saw the sap of Athelu leaking from its roots, and then his vision peered far outwards in the forest, and he felt terror and pain, and the many voices the mycelium spoke to Dallin with saying "Athelu." To Calder it instead spoke first the word "Nidhogg." Then again it spoke saying, "Destroyer." From there his dream ended, and he slept through the night peacefully. But during the night his body had changed, the mycelium net accepted him as the leader of the Agaricans, as was the mycelium's right. And Calder became the first true Sovereign of the Shroomen in an age. This made him taller, his body stronger, his cap shine like gem, and gave him a true right to command. Even still, when he woke up he didn't even notice his changes, save for the knowledge beamed into his head like the other Shroomen. He was too focused on "Nidhogg", it scared him deeply, and he wished to speak to someone about it so he went to find Dallin which he found at the top of a hill looking down at Athelu. Approaching he spoke saying,

"Dallin! I wish to speak with you." Astonished by Calder's new form Dallin almost ignored what he said, but drew his attention back to Calder's words and said, "What would you have of me friend?" "In my sleep the mycelium spoke to me, I think it was trying to warn me of something. I heard hundreds of voices call out to me, and told me the words Nidhogg then destroyer. It was eerie and foreboding but the mycelium could not, or did not show me what it was. Since you came here before me, I wonder if you've seen anything strange you haven't mentioned?" Immediately Dallin thought of that trail of destruction he found in the woods, "Yes actually, I almost had the fortune of forgetting it because of all the recent events. I found a trail through the forest that seemed as if a large creature had barreled through in a rampage. In the middle of the destruction I found this large scale." Dallin proceeded to hand the scale to Calder, and Calder looked at him and said, "This seems less like a scale and more like a metal plate, look at how it sheens in the light. I pray this was just some sort of beast or and not the destroyer the mycelium spoke of. Still though this could be useful, thank you Dallin I think I'll be on my way." "Let me know if you need anything else then, I'll be here." "There is one thing, actually. Tell the people to start producing weaponry and food as soon as possible, we'll need to defend ourselves from whatever this being is I'm sure of it."

Dallin nodded and swiftly went to do so, while Calder journeyed back to the foot of Athelu to ponder the scale. In fact, he was so focused on the scale that he stumbled on a rock, which allowed it to gouge into his thumb gouging out purple blood. Irritated he pocketed the scale into an old red leather bag, and he tore a small piece of cloth from his tunic to cover his wound. Then he began to walk closer to the tree examining it closely. It's red bark was smooth and emanated power, and its roots like a tap had sap flowing into the ground. Enamored by it, and somewhat carelessly he ran his hand beneath the sap to drink it, but before he did he noticed his thumb no longer hurt. Confused he unwrapped the cloth surrounding his finger and saw that the wound had disappeared. In response he unbuckled his leather bag and grabbed the scale and cut his thumb again, purposefully this time, and ran his finger under the sap. The wound closed again in front of his very eyes and he spoke to himself,

"The sap! It's the sap keeping the forest alive it must be!" He bolted his glowing yellow eyes to a few passers by and said, "You three! come here." They dropped what they were doing and came over to calder, and one of them spoke up saying "Yes my king?" "Grab some of the sap from this tree, store in a container, and bring it outside of the forest, and pour it on the dying grass. Once you do this tell me what happens, understood?" The three Agaricans look at each other in confusion, but knew they weren't to argue and another one of them spoke saying, "Yes, of course. We'll do that now" "Good, and be swift! This may be vital."

The three went to do as Calder asked, and Calder paced around the tree thinking of many things. He wondered that if the sap could heal him, perhaps it could also heal plants. He thought that perhaps using that sap The Agaricans could heal the world, negate what The Rain has done, and help all the others who must be suffering without a land just like Gadfyrth. He thought this must be why the Athelu was placed here, that it must've been a divine work and that he and his people must have had some sort of duty to fulfill. He was struck with even more fear of the thing named Nidhogg, for if that thing meant to destroy Athelu, the world may very well lay in its ruined state forever. While all these thoughts sat in his mind, he kneeled at the foot of the tree and for the rest of that day he spent his time in prayer. Eventually late in the night the three Agaricans returned home and with good news; they told Calder the sap seeped into the dirt and made grass sprout out from the ground there, and within a large area of where the sap was placed. It was as Calder theorized and he rejoiced and turned to the three saying,

"You have all done a great deed in doing this for me. Now tell me, what are your names?" The one that had spoken the most up to that point had a tall and vibrant cap and said, "My name is Domweald my king." Another one spoke who was slightly short and had a flatter cap saying, "My name's Oslaf m'lord." The third who had an unremarkable cap was tall and strong looking for an Agarican then said, "Sigeheard is my name." Calder nodded and said, "You three will tend to the sap of Athelu, I want you to collect a force and construct a very large cellar. Once the cellar is built I want you to make barrels, pots, anything to store large amounts of sap, and I want you to fill the cellar with whatever you use. Once one cellar is filled I want you to repeat the process, build on cellar after another next to each other." Calder looked around and saw the same hill he talked to Dallin on earlier and pointed at it, "That hill, hollow it out as a start. If you do this I will consider you nobles, and you will be rewarded handsomely."

To that they agreed, but decided to sleep first, and so did Calder. During Calder's sleep the mycelium spoke to him again, and said in its many voices, "Speak. Command. Organize." And showed him imagery in his head of his people sleeping. He did as the mycelium said and attempted speak, and then found that he could in a sense speak. It felt as if he sent thoughts, and received understanding from hundreds at a time, and he instinctively used his ability to communicate to his people what next to do. He told them to make tools and houses, farm, hunt, and all the sorts of things they'd need to build their civilization back up again before he quickly fell back into deep sleep. Once Calder awoke he already saw his people hard at work, the foundations of houses and other buildings like storehouses were built, the cellar had partially been dug, and some folk were seen carrying deer over their shoulders. This would be the start of the capital of Agaricans, and Calder named his home Dunburg.
